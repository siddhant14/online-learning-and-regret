Online Learning
and Regret

Prediction with expert advice and the Weighted Majority algorithm

Why I chose this topic

I've worked with ML models and trading signals before, so I'm interested in how learning algorithms perform when data arrives sequentially and predictions have to be made without knowing what comes next. I want to explore what guarantees we can make even when the data is unpredictable or adversarial.

Proposed outline

  1. Introduction to online learning

    I’ll start with a prediction problem where data arrives one round at a time. A simple example will show what the learner knows before making a prediction and what it learns afterward.

  2. Prediction with expert advice

    I’ll introduce the expert-advice framework and define predictions, losses, and cumulative loss. I’ll then explain the comparison with the best expert in hindsight, using a small example.

  3. The Weighted Majority algorithm

    I’ll describe how the algorithm combines expert predictions and reduces the weights of experts that make mistakes. I’ll work through a few rounds to show how the weights change, then discuss a bound on the algorithm’s mistakes.

  4. Randomized prediction and regret

    I’ll introduce randomized weighted prediction and define expected regret. This section will state a regret bound relative to the best fixed expert and discuss the role of the learning rate.

  5. Proof of the regret bound

    I’ll prove the bound using the total weight of the experts as a potential function. The proof will compare upper and lower bounds on this quantity, then use them to bound regret and choose a learning rate.

  6. Examples and experiments

    I’ll use stable, alternating, and changing outcome sequences to compare the algorithms. I’ll also discuss what the guarantees do and do not say, including the assumptions about feedback and the choice of comparator.
