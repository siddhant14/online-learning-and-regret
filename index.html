02The prediction gameExperts · binary predictions · loss · hindsight comparator+

This section will formalize the prediction-with-expert-advice protocol and its notation. A worked round will lead to cumulative loss and the best-fixed-expert comparator.

Author explanation pending
  • Define N, T, expert predictions, outcomes, and 0–1 loss.
  • Walk through one round with a small table.
  • Define cumulative loss and regret against one fixed expert over the entire sequence.
03Weighted MajorityInitialization · weighted vote · multiplicative penalties+

This section will present deterministic Weighted Majority, including initialization, the voting rule, and multiplicative weight updates. A short trace and a mistake guarantee will accompany the algorithm.

Author explanation pending
  • Explain equal starting weights and the penalty factor β = exp(−η).
  • Explain the deterministic vote and the tie rule.
  • Trace a mistake and the next weight update.
  • State a mistake bound; distinguish it from an additive sublinear regret guarantee.
w[i] = 1 for each expert i
for each round t:
    predict 1 if weighted vote for 1 ≥ 1/2, else 0
    observe y[t] and all expert losses
    w[i] = w[i] * exp(−η * loss[i,t])
04Randomized advice & regretHedge · expected loss · learning-rate tradeoff+

This section will introduce randomized weighted prediction and expected regret. It will state a regret theorem and examine how the learning rate changes its guarantee.

Author explanation pending
  • Replace the majority vote with sampling an expert from normalized weights.
  • Explain why the expected round loss is the weighted average of expert losses.
  • State the regret theorem with its assumptions and explain the choice of η.
  • Explain why small regret does not imply small absolute loss.
RT ≤ ln(N)/η + ηT/8
Planned theorem: full-information exponential weights; losses in [0,1]; fixed η > 0. The lab uses binary expert losses and tracks their weighted average.
05The potential-function proofTotal weight · upper and lower bounds · sublinear regret+

This section will prove the regret theorem using the total expert weight as a potential. It will combine upper and lower bounds, choose a learning rate, and interpret the resulting asymptotic guarantee.

Author proof pending
  1. Define total weight W at the beginning of each round.
  2. Lower-bound the final total weight using the best fixed expert.
  3. Use Hoeffding’s lemma to upper-bound each log weight ratio.
  4. Sum the inequalities, combine bounds, and rearrange.
  5. Optimize η for a known horizon T and interpret RT/T.
06Experiments & limitationsStable experts · regime changes · adversarial outcomes+

This section will connect the theory to the interactive lab through stable, switching, and adversarial examples. It will discuss the fixed comparator, full-information feedback, and the limits of the guarantee.

Author discussion pending
  • Compare the stable signal and switching winner in the lab.
  • Compare deterministic voting with expected randomized loss against the vote-punishing sequence.
  • Explain a fixed comparator versus an expert allowed to switch each round.
  • Discuss full feedback, unknown horizon, and the difference between a simulation and a theorem.

WHAT IS MEASURED

Hedge displays the weighted average of the experts’ 0–1 losses, equal to randomized prediction’s conditional expected loss. No random expert is sampled.

COMPARATOR & FEEDBACK

Best fixed expert = minimum cumulative expert loss, not a fresh expert chosen each round. All expert losses are revealed after every round. Regret can be negative.
