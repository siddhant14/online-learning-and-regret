From Online Learning
to Randomized Lower Bounds

What this covers

We’ll start with a prediction problem: several experts give advice, and we have to decide whom to follow before seeing the outcome. Exponential weights gives us a regret bound against the best fixed expert. We’ll prove that bound, use it to prove minimax, and then apply Yao’s principle to show that a randomized algorithm for OR still needs linearly many input queries.

The connection is that all three settings involve choosing among many options against an opponent. The regret argument lets us move from learning to games, and from games to lower bounds.
