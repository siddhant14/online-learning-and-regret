Online Learning
and Regret

From expert advice to lower bounds for randomized algorithms

What this covers

We begin with prediction using expert advice and the exponential-weights algorithm. A potential-function argument bounds its regret against the best fixed expert. We then use low regret against best responses to prove the finite minimax theorem. Applying minimax to a matrix of deterministic algorithms and inputs gives Yao’s principle. Finally, a hard input distribution proves that a bounded-error randomized algorithm for OR must read linearly many input bits.

The two parts follow one argument: combine choices with low regret, turn that guarantee into minimax, then interpret the game as a computation problem.

Before you start

The assumed background is basic probability, expectation, asymptotic notation, and bounded-error randomized computation from 455. Experts, mixed strategies, and decision trees are introduced as they are needed. No ML or game-theory course is required. A short supporting lemma uses elementary differentiation; the main proofs use finite sums and inequalities.
